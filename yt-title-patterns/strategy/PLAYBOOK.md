| Signal | Rescue | Restart |
|---|---|---|

| Any video > 10K views | yes | no |
| One clear topic ≥ 70% of uploads | yes | no (mixed uploads) |

watch history/comments have zero transfer value but deleting gains nothing),
and launch fresh channels with clean positioning. **A confused channel identity
is the one thing YouTube's recommendation system never forgives** — the
algorithm learns *who to show your videos to* from your existing audience, and
a mixed-topic channel teaches it nothing.

## 3. The MrBeast method, distilled to what's copyable

Backed by the dataset (`data/analysis.json`):

1. **Package first, produce second.** Title + thumbnail are decided *before*
   the video is made. If the title isn't clickable, the idea dies. Every video
   in our 1M+ dataset is a one-sentence pitch.
2. **Concrete numbers beat adjectives.** 35% of all 1M+ titles contain a
   number; titles with huge numbers ($ amounts, "100,000,000") hit 160M median
   views vs 45M overall.
3. **Stakes in the title.** Dollar amounts (160M median), challenge/survival
   framing (140M), time limits — "50 Hours", "30 Days" — (120M). For
   non-stunt niches the *transferable* version is: a measurable claim with a
   visible outcome ("I Practiced CBT On Myself For 30 Days").
4. **5–8 words.** The dataset's top performers cluster there. Front-load the
   hook — YouTube truncates around 50–60 characters.
5. **One repeatable format, iterated.** Ping Pong Trick Shots 1→4, Glitter Bomb
   1.0→4.0, Penny Series Day 1→30. Find your format that works, then version
   it. Series markers appear in 12% of the dataset.
6. **First 30 seconds = re-state the title's promise, visually.** Retention is
   the ranking currency; the intro proves the click was right.
7. **One audience per channel.** Never teach the algorithm two audiences on one
   channel (the reason for §2).
8. **Volume with a floor.** Weekly long-form + 3–5 Shorts. Never ship below
   the packaging bar to hit cadence.

## 4. Channel 1 — PHIR Mind (Global Mental Health & CBT)

**Positioning:** evidence-based psychology explained by a clinician — Veritasium
energy, clinical rigor. Three audience tiers, one channel: lead with
general-public packaging (the big numbers live there), deliver
professional-grade content inside, and put clinician-only material in a
dedicated playlist until it earns its own channel.

**Formats:**
- *Flagship (weekly, 8–15 min):* one psychological mechanism, myth, or
  technique, packaged as a claim or experiment.
- *Technique series (biweekly):* "CBT Techniques That Actually Work — #1
  Cognitive Restructuring" — the versioned-series play.
- *Shorts (3–5/wk):* one reframe, one 60-second technique, one myth-bust —
  cut from the flagship.

**Launch titles (data-backed patterns in brackets):**
1. "The 5-Minute Technique Therapists Use On Themselves" [number + time + curiosity]
2. "Why Your Brain Won't Let You Stop Worrying" [Why-opener + negation]
3. "I Practiced CBT On Myself For 30 Days" [first-person + time limit]
4. "The Thinking Error 90% of People Make Daily" [huge number + claim]
5. "Therapy Myths Your Therapist Wishes You'd Stop Believing" [negation + insider]
6. "How Psychologists Actually Treat Panic Attacks" [How-opener + insider]
7. "The World's Most Effective Therapy, Explained" [superlative + explainer]
8. "3 Questions That Rewire Negative Thoughts" [number + outcome]
9. "What 10,000 Therapy Sessions Taught Me About Anxiety" [huge number + first-person]
10. "Depression Isn't What You Think It Is" [negation + curiosity]

**Compliance guardrails (non-negotiable):** education-not-therapy disclaimer in
every description; no diagnosing viewers; crisis-line links pinned; anonymize
all clinical material beyond recognition; check your licensing board's
social-media guidance. These protect the channel *and* your license.

**Monetization ladder:** AdSense (psychology RPM ~$6–14, among YouTube's
highest) → lead magnet (free CBT worksheet pack, builds email list) → paid:
professional CE-style workshops, client-facing course, supervision/consulting.
Realistic milestones: 1K subs + monetization at month 3–6 with weekly cadence;
100K in 18–36 months if 2–3 videos break out. Income at 100K subs in this
niche: roughly $2–6K/month AdSense + typically 2–5× that from your own
products.

## 5. Channel 2 — Scripture Sessions (biblical music)

**Different physics — this is the honest part.** Music channels were *excluded*
from our scrape dataset for a reason: music grows through **repeat listens,
playlists, search, and Shorts audio reuse**, not through MrBeast-style
click-packaging. Cloning the MrBeast strategy onto a music channel fails; what
transfers is consistency, branding, and series thinking.

**What works for worship/scripture music:**
- *Lyric videos* (the workhorse — cheap, watchable, searchable: "Psalm 91 song",
  "scripture songs for sleep" have durable search volume).
- *Themed compilations/loops:* "1 Hour of Scripture Songs for Anxiety" — long
  watch-time magnets, the music equivalent of a flagship.
- *Series identity:* "Scripture Sessions #12: Psalm 23" — versioned like Ping
  Pong Trick Shots.
- *Shorts:* 30–60s hook sections of each track; worship Shorts audio gets
  reused, which is free distribution.
- Release cadence: 2 tracks + 1 compilation/month minimum; batch-produce in
  Suno.

**Two hard warnings:**
1. **Suno licensing:** commercial use of Suno output requires a paid plan
   (outputs made on the free tier aren't yours to monetize). Verify your plan
   before uploading monetized tracks.
2. **YouTube's inauthentic-content policy** (tightened July 2025) demonetizes
   *mass-produced, low-transformation AI content*. Pure auto-generated track +
   static image at volume is exactly the pattern it targets. Protection: add
   genuine human layers — your own lyric writing from scripture, visual craft,
   on-camera framing/devotional intros, your story. That's also what makes it
   *worth subscribing to*.

**Monetization:** AdSense is weak in music (RPM ~$1–3); the real ladder is
distribution (DistroKid/TuneCore → Spotify/Apple), sync licensing, and the
channel as the audience engine for everything else.

4. "What 1,000 AI Generations Taught Me About Prompting" [huge number]
5. "The AI Art Workflow Nobody Shows You" [negation + insider]

Tech-niche RPM ~$5–15. Same weekly + Shorts cadence.

## 7. 90-day execution plan (channel 1 first)

**Days 1–14 — Foundation.** Run the §1 Studio check. Create PHIR Mind: name,
handle, banner, about page, disclaimer template. Write 20 titles using §4
patterns; pick the 6 with the strongest thumbnail potential (if you can't
sketch the thumbnail in 30 seconds, the packaging is weak — kill it).
**Days 15–45 — First six.** Batch-record 6 flagships, cut 3 Shorts from each.
Publish 1 flagship + 3 Shorts weekly. Same-day replies to every comment.
**Days 46–90 — Read data, double down.** In Studio: CTR (target >4%), 30-second
retention (>70%), avg view duration (>40%). Take the best performer and version
it into a series (§3, rule 5). Start the Scripture Sessions build in parallel
only if you're sustaining cadence without quality slipping.

**KPIs that matter (in order):** retention → CTR → subs-per-video → RPM.
Views are an output, not a lever.
